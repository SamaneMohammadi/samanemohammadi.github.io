About

I am a final-year PhD candidate in the School of Informatics at the University of Edinburgh, advised by Dr. Marc Juarez. My work focuses on trustworthy AI/ML, in particular privacy, fairness and security of machine learning systems.

During my PhD, my research has developed along three threads:

  • Scalable privacy preservation: protecting distributed learning, where a model is trained collaboratively across many devices rather than on centrally pooled data, in a way that stays practical at scale.
  • Private machine unlearning: letting participants remove their data’s influence from a distributed model without relying on a central server.
  • Privacy–fairness tensions: designing group-aware architectures that give equitable performance across demographic groups under differential privacy guarantees.

A common thread across all of it is making trustworthiness guarantees hold in realistic settings: at scale, across heterogeneous devices, and without trusting a central party.

Recently, as an associate researcher at the University of Edinburgh, I started working on the trustworthiness of LLM-based systems, especially their fairness and security, in high-stakes settings such as hiring, in collaboration with Amazon.

Ongoing

Bias and fairness in large language models (LLMs)

Ongoing

School of Informatics, The University of Edinburgh · Edinburgh, United Kingdom

Bias in an LLM is not spread evenly across the network but concentrated in particular components. This work first locates which attention heads and feed-forward layers carry it, then fine-tunes only those components against a fairness-aware training objective.

Trustworthiness in LLM-based systems for high-stakes decisions

Ongoing

School of Informatics, The University of Edinburgh · Edinburgh, United Kingdom · In collaboration with Amazon

LLM-based systems are increasingly deployed in high-stakes domains such as hiring pipelines, where their decisions directly affect people. This project studies how trustworthy these systems are in such settings. It is funded by and carried out in collaboration with Amazon.
